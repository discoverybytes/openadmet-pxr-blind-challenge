# Activity Prediction — Project Summary

**Challenge**: [OpenADMET PXR Induction Challenge](https://huggingface.co/spaces/openadmet/pxr-challenge)

## Data Curation

The challenge provided 4,140 dose-response pEC50 measurements. Curation proceeded in
two stages, reducing the training set to 3,386 compounds.

**Reactive electrophile exclusion**. Curation identified 754 compounds containing potentially reactive functional groups (acrylamides n=236, acrylates n=229, aldehydes n=87). The test set contains
none of these compounds. Excluding them improved cross-validation RAE by approximately
0.019.

**PAINS/REOS filtering** was evaluated and rejected. Removing 841 compounds
that failed PAINS or REOS alerts reduced a LightGBM model from OOF RAE 0.609 to 0.724.
Despite being "poor" compounds, they anchor the inactive end of the activity landscape and provide chemical diversity that aids model calibration. 

**Sample weighting**: 474 compounds were assigned reduced weights (0.3–0.5) for high
measurement uncertainty, extreme molecular weight, or very low activity with no structural
neighbours in the test set. Three compounds were removed entirely due to zero structural
similarity to any test compound.

**Stereochemistry**: 39.5% of training and 38.2% of test compounds have undefined
stereocenters. The matched distributions meant filtering would have biased training
relative to test. All undefined-stereocenter compounds were retained as-is.

**Tautomer corrections**: Three test compounds had probably incorrect tautomeric forms in the
challenge SMILES (enol forms instead of keto). Edited SMILES were re-run through
Chemprop and the prediction shifts applied as offsets to the final ensemble (−0.356,
−0.411, and −0.009 pEC50 units respectively). Uni-Mol and MolE were not re-run, as
3D and embedding-based representations are substantially less sensitive to tautomeric
form. These corrections contributed approximately 0.002 RAE improvement.

## What We Tried

### Chemprop Architecture Variants

The first models were single-task Chemprop D-MPNN trained on the primary pEC50
measurements alone. Regularisation (dropout, weight decay, early stopping) was added
in a v2 run but produced no meaningful improvement: test RAE remained ~0.62.

**Foundation weights**: Switching from random initialisation to CheMeleon pretrained
weights was the single largest improvement of the project. Using it as a starting point for
PXR fine-tuning reduced test RAE from ~0.62 to ~0.59.

**Multi-task training**: Training jointly on four related assays consistently outperformed
the single-task approach. The four targets were the primary pEC50, PXR-null
counter-assay, single-concentration screen, and FXR activity as a cross-receptor regulariser. A two-task
versus four-task comparison on identical compounds showed a 0.070 RAE advantage for
the four-task configuration.

**MAE loss**: Switching the training objective from MSE to MAE improved gradient signal
on the noisy activity labels and reduced model sensitivity to outliers, providing a
further ~0.01 RAE gain.

**3D shape descriptors (PMI)**: 19 principal-moment-of-inertia descriptors computed from
RDKit conformers were added as molecule-level features. These capture global 3D shape
(rod-like, disc-like, spherical) orthogonally to the 2D message-passing graph.

The combination of CheMeleon weights, four-task multitask training, MAE loss, and PMI
descriptors produced the final Chemprop configuration (JG_MAE), with standalone OOF
RAE = 0.5810.

### LightGBM Variants

Multiple LightGBM configurations were evaluated as standalone models and potential
ensemble members:

- **ECFP4 fingerprints**: OOF RAE 0.635. Test prediction standard deviation was
  compressed to 44% of training standard deviation, which hurt ensemble performance
  when blended with the GNN.
- **Mordred descriptors**: Best LightGBM standalone (OOF RAE 0.574), but test RAE
  was 0.67 — a large OOF-to-test gap indicating the Mordred features overfit to
  training-set scaffolds.
- **ECFP4 + 3D descriptors**: OOF RAE 0.609, similar compression issue.
- **Multiple fingerprint types combined**: OOF RAE 0.609, no improvement over ECFP4
  alone.

Pure LightGBM variants were ruled out as standalone submissions and as ensemble
components. Despite competitive cross-validation scores, they consistently failed to
generalise to the novel scaffolds in the test set — a pattern observed across all
feature-based models without large-scale molecular pretraining.

### Alternative Model Architectures

**Uni-Mol**: A 3D molecular transformer pretrained on 209 million conformers. Fine-tuned
on the PXR training set using a single conformer per molecule. At inference, predictions
were averaged across 10 randomised SMILES representations per compound — different atom
orderings produce different RDKit conformers, reducing prediction variance. Standalone
OOF RAE ~0.645. Despite weaker standalone performance than Chemprop, Uni-Mol's 3D
geometric representations provide genuinely complementary signal and transferred well to
the test set.

**MolE + LightGBM**: MolE is a molecular foundation model pretrained on large-scale
unlabelled chemical data. Its fixed embeddings fed into a LightGBM adaptor achieved OOF
RAE ~0.705 — the weakest of the three final models. However, it provided architectural
diversity and strong test generalisation via the pretrained embeddings.

**MolFormer**: An IBM SMILES-based transformer (ibm/MolFormer-XL-both-10pct). Completely
failed: OOF Spearman rho = −0.007 — worse than random ordering. Ruled out.

**TabPFN**: A tabular transformer that performs in-context learning without gradient
training. Tested with multiple input types:
- Raw Morgan fingerprints / Mordred descriptors: standalone OOF RAE > 0.70 — far too
  weak to contribute.
- CheMeleon learned embeddings: superficially promising (Ridge coefficient 0.205 in
  a stack), but revealed a fundamental flaw: embeddings from OOF-fold-trained models
  differ systematically from embeddings generated with all training data, creating a
  distribution shift that degrades test performance. Ruled out.

**TabPFN v2 on PMI features**: slightly better than v1 variants but still marginal
(Ridge coefficient 0.068, Δ0.0003 OOF improvement). Not submitted.

### Ensemble and Post-Processing Experiments

**Diverse seed ensemble**: Training 5+ independently seeded Chemprop models and averaging
was tested but found to add minimal value — seed-to-seed correlations were too high
(rho > 0.97) to provide meaningful ensemble diversity.

**Isotonic calibration**: Applied post-hoc recalibration using a held-out validation
split. Abandoned after finding that prediction compression is expected for
scaffold-split-trained models (the test set scaffolds are harder than any validation fold)
and calibrating toward a scaffold-adjacent validation split did not improve test
performance.

**Variance scaling**: Multiplied test predictions by k = 1.10 to expand the dynamic range.
Made things worse: test RAE worsened by +0.01 and Spearman decreased. The compression
is a real phenomenon but scaling is the wrong fix.

**Knowledge distillation**: The ensemble (Chemprop + Uni-Mol + MolE) is more accurate
than any single model. The idea here was to use the ensemble's predictions as training
labels for a new Chemprop model — effectively teaching Chemprop what the full ensemble
knows. The new model was then combined with Uni-Mol and MolE in a fresh ensemble.

This backfired. Because the new Chemprop had been trained on predictions that already
incorporated Uni-Mol and MolE, it was no longer independent of them — it had absorbed
their signal during training. When Ridge regression weighted the three models, it found
Uni-Mol and MolE were now redundant and slashed their weights. The ensemble lost
diversity and performance regressed (ΔRAE = +0.0126). The lesson: training any model
on another model's predictions contaminates the independence that makes ensembling work.

## Where We Ended Up

**Final submitted model**: JG_MAE + Uni-Mol aug10 + MolE+LightGBM, combined via
Ridge regression meta-learner fitted on out-of-fold predictions.

Ridge coefficients: Chemprop (JG_MAE) = 0.706, MolE = 0.206, Uni-Mol = 0.166,
intercept = −0.383.

| Model | Standalone OOF RAE |
|-------|--------------------|
| Chemprop (JG_MAE) | 0.5810 |
| Uni-Mol aug10 | ~0.645 |
| MolE + LightGBM | ~0.705 |
| **Ridge ensemble (v49)** | **0.5692 OOF → 0.5380 test** |


The OOF→test generalisation gain of 0.031 RAE (predictions improved relative to OOF
estimate on the blinded test set) reflects a consistent transductive effect observed
across the project: models trained for the test distribution — even marginally — benefit
more than their OOF scores suggest.

## Phase 2: Unblinded Analysis and Final Submission

Midway through the competition, data was released for half of the test set. Scoring simultaneously switched to the full 513-compound set.

**Assessment of v49 on the 253 unblinded compounds**: MAE=0.428, RAE=0.536, systematic bias +0.09 (mild overprediction). Ninety percent of predictions fell within 1.0 pEC50 unit.

**Floor problem**: The principal failure was systematic overprediction of truly inactive compounds. A small cohort of compounds with very low true activity were predicted at 4.0–4.7, errors of +2.0 to +2.6 units. These inactives were structurally similar to highly active training compounds (Tanimoto 0.50–0.58; nearest training neighbours had pEC50 5.8–6.8) — the model recognised familiar scaffolds and over-predicted activity. Adding these compounds to the training set was the primary fix available.

**Retraining**: Chemprop (JG_MAE) and Uni-Mol were retrained on the expanded 3,639-compound dataset (3,386 original + 253 unblinded). The Ridge stack was refitted on the original 3,386-compound out-of-fold predictions to preserve a clean validation. MolE was not retrained — its embeddings are frozen and retraining only the LightGBM adaptor on 253 additional points gives minimal gain.

**Tautomer patch**: One compound (OADMET-0006514) had an unblinded true pEC50 of 2.725. Despite the tautomer correction, the model continued to predict 3.53 because the incorrect tautomeric SMILES remained in the training set and the delta offset was insufficient. The final submission value was replaced directly with the known true value.

**Final submission**: JG_MAE_v2 + Uni-Mol_v2 + MolE+LightGBM, Ridge stack. OOF RAE on the original 3,386 compounds: 0.567.
